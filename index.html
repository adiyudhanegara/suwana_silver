Suwana Silver

Handmade silver from a family workshop in Celuk, since 1997.

Three generations, one workbench.

Made Suwana's grandfather was the first silversmith in the family. He worked at a charcoal block in the family compound in Celuk, making silver for ceremonies and for neighbors in the village.

Everything by hand.

  1. 01DesignThe piece is drawn and the motif chosen: a flower, a lotus, a Barong.
  2. 02Melt925 silver is melted in a clay crucible and poured into a bar.
  3. 03Draw wireThe bar is pulled through a draw plate until the wire is thin enough.

Grain by grain.

  1. 04Shape filigreeFine wire is twisted and bent into the motif with tweezers.
  2. 05SolderEach joint is soldered on the charcoal block with a small torch.
  3. 06PolishThe piece is cleaned, filed and polished by hand.

The family and the shop

Made grew up next to that workbench. He learned by watching first, then by doing: sorting wire, fanning the charcoal, and finally holding the torch himself.

In 1997 he opened Suwana Silver, a jewelry shop built on the same bench and the same techniques.

The tools are a little newer now. The work is still done by hand, one piece at a time, in the workshop behind the shop.

Today visitors can sit at that bench too, and leave with a ring they made themselves.
